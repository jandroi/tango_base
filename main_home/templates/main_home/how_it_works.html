How It Works

Structure behind decisions — so the same question produces the same answer.

Engagement

The engagement path

Most clients walk a ladder. You can enter at any rung.

  1. 01

    Assessment

    A gap analysis on your data, decisions, and reporting — measuring where you are against where you should be.

  2. 02

    Blueprint

    The design of the target data structure — the hierarchy, definitions, and rules that close the gaps surfaced in Assessment.

  3. 03

    Implementation

    Executing the Blueprint — turning the target structure into a working system. Delivery mode scoped to your team's capacity at engagement start.

  4. 04

    Fractional CDO

    Monthly custodianship of the data structure — part-time, multi-month, covering strategy through ongoing operations as your organization grows.

  5. 05

    Training

    A focused engagement to transfer Tango's method to your team — so they can operate, extend, and maintain the structure without ongoing Tango dependency.

Foundation

What we stabilize first

Before results change, the structure underneath decisions is made explicit.

Unreliable decisions usually come from definitions, inputs, and rules that exist informally or implicitly.

01

Definitions

We agree on what key numbers mean and how they should come to be.

This removes ambiguity and identifies the sources before any reporting takes place.

02

Inputs

We identify which data points are required to produce each number, including inputs that are missing, assumed, or currently handled manually.

03

Rules

We centralize the logic that turns inputs into metrics, so it exists once and is reused consistently, rather than recreated in each spreadsheet or report.

In practice

What changes in practice

Stable decision inputs

Consistent numbers

Key figures stay the same across meetings, tools, and reports.

Explicit definitions

Metrics, rules, and assumptions are documented and shared, not inferred from context or memory.

Traceable answers

When a number is questioned, there is a path back to the source: what was used, how it was calculated, and why.

Reduced manual dependency

Decisions stop relying on individual files, personal knowledge, or whoever prepared the last version.

Where this applies

This work matters most when:

Decisions are repeated, not one-off

Numbers are questioned, not just reported

Manual steps exist but no one fully owns them

The cost of being wrong is higher than the cost of being slower
